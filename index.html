Welcome to Pro-FRS

This tool helps in the preliminary assessment of road maintenance needs by calculating the Proactive Feeder Road Scheduler (Pro-FRS) based on various road conditions, environmental factors, and traffic data. It provides insights into the current state of road segments and suggests potential maintenance strategies.

About This Tool

▼

This Proactive Feeder Road Scheduler (Pro-FRS) calculates the optimal maintenance interval for unpaved feeder roads based on the research conducted in Rulindo District, Rwanda. The tool implements a locally calibrated predictive formula developed through field studies and empirical data analysis.

Research Background: This tool is part of a final year project at Rwanda Polytechnic/Musanze College, Department of Civil Engineering - Highway Technology. The research addresses the challenge of reactive maintenance practices for feeder roads in Rwanda by providing a data-driven approach to maintenance planning.

Methodology: The predictive formula was developed through comprehensive field data collection, including road condition assessments, traffic counts, and climate data analysis. The coefficients were calibrated specifically for Rwandan conditions using empirical observations from the Tumba-Buyoga feeder road in Rulindo District.

Application: This tool is designed for use by road maintenance planners, district engineers, and infrastructure managers in Rwanda to optimize maintenance scheduling and resource allocation for feeder road networks.

Formula Details

▼

Predictive Maintenance Time Formula

T = (100 - (2×R) - (3×(G/100)) - (4×P) - (0.8×P_volume) - (3×D)) / ((0.1×ADT) + (0.05×RF))

Where:

  • T = Time to Maintenance (months)
  • R = Rutting Severity Index (1-5 scale)
  • G = Gravel Loss Percentage (0-100%)
  • P = Pothole Density (number per 100m)
  • P_volume = (Length × Width × Depth × 0.6) / 1000 (in thousands Cm³)
  • D = Drainage Condition Index (1-5 scale)
  • ADT = Average Daily Traffic (vehicles/day)
  • RF = Monthly Rainfall (mm/month)

Coefficient Justification

The coefficients in the formula were determined based on:

  • Potholes (4): Highest coefficient due to immediate safety impact and rapid deterioration acceleration
  • Pothole Volume (0.8): Additional factor accounting for the severity of pothole damage based on volume
  • Drainage (3): Critical factor as poor drainage accelerates all other deterioration mechanisms
  • Gravel Loss (3): Significant impact on road structural integrity and riding quality
  • Rutting (2): Important but less critical than potholes and drainage issues
  • Traffic (0.1): Scaling factor to convert vehicle count to appropriate stress units
  • Rainfall (0.05): Scaling factor for rainfall impact, slightly lower than traffic

Parameter Sources & Methodology

Rutting Severity Index (R)

Source: Field measurements using 1.2m straightedge and graduated ruler at 20m intervals along wheel paths.

Methodology: Based on RS 267-2:2021 (Unpaved Roads - Maintenance) and adapted from AASHTO pavement condition rating systems.

Scale Development: The 1-5 scale was calibrated through field observations on Tumba-Buyoga road, correlating rut depth with vehicle handling impact.

Reference: Rwanda Standards RS 267-2:2021; Paterson, W.D.O. (1987). Road Deterioration and Maintenance Effects.

Gravel Loss (G)

Source: Visual estimation over 10m segments of 100m sample sections.

Methodology: Team consensus approach for percentage estimation, validated with photographic evidence and cross-verification.

Calibration: Correlation with actual gravel volume measurements from sample sections.

Reference: Haas, R., Hudson, W.R., & Zaniewski, J. (1994). Modern Pavement Management; World Bank HDM-4 Model.

Pothole Density (P)

Source: Field count of depressions meeting minimum dimensions (150mm plan, 25mm depth).

Methodology: Standardized counting procedure with chalk marking to avoid duplication.

Definition: Operational definition developed based on safety impact and repair complexity considerations.

Reference: ASTM D6433-07: Standard Practice for Roads and Parking Lots Pavement Condition Index Surveys.

Pothole Volume (P_volume)

Source: Calculated from average dimensions of potholes measured in the field.

Methodology: Volume = (Length × Width × Depth × 0.6) / 1000, where 0.6 is a shape factor accounting for irregular pothole shapes.

Units: Expressed in thousands of cubic centimeters (Cm³) for easier interpretation.

Reference: Developed specifically for Rwandan feeder road conditions based on field measurements.

Drainage Condition Index (D)

Source: Visual assessment of side drains and road surface ponding evidence.

Methodology: Composite rating based on drain functionality, siltation levels, and water persistence after rainfall.

Scale Development: Correlation with observed deterioration rates in different drainage conditions.

Reference: Rwanda Standards RS 267-1:2021 (Unpaved Roads - Design and Construction); Cedergren, H.R. (1974). Drainage of Highway and Airfield Pavements.

Average Daily Traffic (ADT)

Source: 12-hour manual classified traffic counts (6:00 AM - 6:00 PM) over 7 consecutive days.

Methodology: Expansion factors applied to convert 12-hour counts to 24-hour ADT based on vehicle type distributions.

Validation: Cross-checked with district transport office records where available.

Reference: Rwanda Transport Development Agency (RTDA) Traffic Monitoring Guidelines; AASHTO Guide for Traffic Data Programs.

Monthly Rainfall (RF)

Source: Rwanda Meteorology Agency (RMA) historical data for Rulindo District.

Methodology: 5-year monthly averages (2019-2023) to account for seasonal variations.

Processing: Data cleaned for outliers and missing values using linear interpolation.

Reference: Rwanda Meteorology Agency Climate Data; World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal.

Formula Coefficients

Source: Calibrated through literature review and empirical field observations.

Methodology: Relative impact scaling (1-5) based on defect severity and contribution to overall road deterioration.

Validation: Sensitivity analysis and scenario testing against engineering judgment.

Reference: Paterson (1987) deterioration models; Oloo, S.Y. (2017) regression models for gravel roads; HDM-4 calibration studies.
